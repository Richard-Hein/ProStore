## useActionState
Why use useActionState instead of useState?
Before this hook, you had to manually create loading, error, and data states, then write a handleSubmit function with try/catch blocks. useActionState hides that "plumbing," making your component much cleaner and more predictable.

Note: If you are using an older version of React (pre-19) or an older Next.js version, you might have seen this referred to as useFormState. They are effectively the same, but useActionState is the updated, standard name.

## useFormStatus
Basically, these two hooks work together as a team: useActionState tells the user what happened, and useFormStatus tells the user it's happening right now.
